# The Last Call

Ray pulled up to the address at three in the afternoon. A duplex on Morrison Street. Peeling paint and a chain-link fence with no gate. He checked the file again. Tommy Chen, age nine. School absences piling up.

He knocked and waited. Knocked again.

A woman opened the door. Thin, with eyes that moved past him to the street.

"Child Services. I'm here about Tommy."

"He's sick."

"I need to see him."

She hesitated. Ray had done this a hundred times and the hesitation always meant something.

"Just a minute."

She closed the door. He heard voices inside, low and urgent.

The door opened and a man stood there now. Tattoos on his neck. Ray recognized the ink. He'd seen it before, years ago when he wore a badge.

"Tommy's sleeping," the man said.

"I'll wait."

"Come back tomorrow."

Ray looked past him into the dark hallway. Something was wrong. He felt it in his gut the way he used to feel things before the pills took that away.

"I need to see the boy now."

"No."

"State law says I can inspect the home during a welfare check."

The man's jaw tightened. "Wait here."

He closed the door again. Ray heard more voices and then footsteps moving fast through the house. He tried the door. Locked now.

Ray went to the side gate and found it open. He moved along the fence line into the backyard. Dirt and weeds. A shed against the back wall with a new padlock that didn't match the house.

The back door burst open and two men came out. One was the guy from the front door. They had bags in their hands and were moving for the shed. They saw Ray.

"Get out of here," the first man said.

"Where's Tommy?"

The second man pulled a gun. Ray put his hands up and backed toward the fence.

"I'm calling the police."

"Go ahead."

Ray had his phone out when the first shot came. It hit the fence post next to his head and he dropped down. More shots. The men were shooting and moving for the back gate. Ray stayed low behind a rusted wheelbarrow and pulled out his phone.

The men got to the gate and went through it. Ray heard a car start and tires on gravel. He stayed down and made the call. Dispatch connected him to someone. A woman's voice, calm and professional.

"This is Lieutenant Sofia Martinez. What's your location?"

Ray told her and gave her the address. Explained what he saw and that shots were fired.

"Are you injured?"

"No."

"Stay where you are. Unit's on the way."

"There's a kid inside."

"I understand. Do not go back in."

He ended the call and looked at the house. The woman was watching from a window. She saw him and the curtain fell back.

Ray went to the front door and knocked again. No answer. He tried the knob. Still locked. He could hear crying inside. A child's voice.

The sirens were getting closer.

The nightmares came at times like this, when he had to decide and a kid was involved. The boy had been seven, crossing the street after school. Ray never saw him. The pills made everything soft around the edges, everything except the sound of the impact.

He kicked the door. The frame splintered and it swung open.

Inside the house smelled like chemicals and rotting food. He moved down the hallway and called out for Tommy. The crying was coming from upstairs.

He climbed the stairs and his leg ached where the surgery had been, where the injury led to the pills. Found a bedroom with the door locked from outside. He opened it.

Three kids were inside. Tommy was one of them, sitting on a bare mattress. The other two were younger. All of them looked thin and scared.

"It's okay," Ray said. "I'm here to help."

The police came through the door downstairs. Ray heard them shouting and clearing rooms. He led the kids to the hallway and called down.

"Up here. Three children. They're safe."

Officers came up the stairs with guns drawn. One of them lowered his weapon when he saw Ray and the kids.

"You the social worker?"

"Yeah."

"Jesus. You shouldn't have gone in alone."

"I know."

More units arrived and Ray took the kids downstairs and outside. Social workers from his department showed up. Ray handed the children over and gave them what information he had. Then he waited by his car.

Someone was walking toward him across the yard. A woman in a tactical vest over a white shirt. Dark hair pulled back and she moved with the kind of authority that made people step aside. Athletic build, the vest couldn't hide it. She walked like she'd earned every step of ground she'd ever walked.

"I'm Lieutenant Martinez."

"Ray Tillman."

"You went in." Her voice was level but her eyes were dark, tired, and sharp enough to cut.

"There was a kid inside. Kids."

"After I told you not to."

"Yeah."

"They got away because of you."

"The kids didn't."

She looked at him for a long moment and something shifted in her face. "Come on. You can give your statement at the station."

He rode with her. The car was clean and smelled like coffee. She drove fast but smooth, her hands sure on the wheel. She didn't talk and Ray looked out the window, aware of the small space between them.

"I know who you are," she said finally.

"Most people do."

"You were a good cop. Before."

"Before."

"You got clean."

"Four years."

She nodded. "That's good."

At the station she led him to an interview room and brought him coffee. It was bad coffee but he drank it. She sat across from him and pulled out a notebook.

"Tell me what you saw."

He told her. She wrote it down and asked questions. Ray answered. Her handwriting was neat and precise.

"The tattoo you saw," she said. "You recognized it."

"Yeah. Los Trece. I worked a case eight years ago. Before everything."

"This is bigger than we thought."

She closed the notebook and looked at him. Something in her face had changed.

"We've been tracking them. Suspected they were running a fentanyl operation. But kids working the site. That's new."

"Trafficked?"

"Probably. We'll find out."

Ray felt the old anger. The kind that used to drive him. Before the pills made everything flat.

"What happens to Tommy?"

"Foster care. Unless we find family."

"His file says his mom died last year. No other relatives."

Sofia nodded. "Then foster care."

Ray stood. His leg was stiff and she noticed.

"You okay?"

"Old injury."

"The one that started it."

He looked at her and she didn't flinch.

"Yeah."

"You good now?"

"Most days."

She handed him a card. "We'll need you to testify when this goes to trial."

He took the card. Their fingers touched briefly and he felt something he couldn't name, something he hadn't felt in years.

"Call me if you remember anything else," she said.

He left.

---

Two days later she called him.

"We found the main site. Warehouse in Vernon. Twelve kids. All under fifteen."

"Jesus."

"We're moving tonight. I wanted you to know."

"Why?"

"You started this. Thought you'd want to see it through."

He met her outside the warehouse at midnight. She was in full tactical gear with helmet, vest, and rifle. She looked different and harder.

"You should stay in the car," she said.

"You keep saying that."

"I keep meaning it."

"How many inside?"

"Six we know of. Maybe more."

"Armed?"

"Assume yes."

He watched her team gather. Twelve officers and they moved like a machine. Sofia gave orders and they listened. Ray could see why she made lieutenant. She had command.

"Stay behind the line," she told him. "I mean it this time."

The team went in and Ray waited. Heard shouting and then gunfire. His heart was pounding. He thought about the boy he'd hit and about Tommy in that shed and about the twelve kids inside.

He crossed the line.

Inside was chaos with officers shouting and people running. The warehouse was huge with machinery everywhere and a chemical smell in the air. Ray moved along the wall toward where he'd heard the gunfire.

He saw Sofia pinned down behind a forklift. Two shooters had her in a crossfire from the catwalks above. Her team was on the other side of the warehouse and couldn't get to her.

Ray looked around and saw a breaker box on the wall. He got to it and pulled the main switch. The warehouse went dark except for emergency lights.

The shooters turned toward where the sound came from. Sofia moved. Two quick shots and they both went down.

The main lights came back on. Sofia's team secured the area and she walked over to Ray.

"You shouldn't be here."

"You were pinned down."

"I had it under control."

"Didn't look like it."

She studied him for a moment. "Thank you."

He nodded.

"There's a room in back," one of the officers called out. "Door's locked."

Ray followed Sofia to the room. They breached it and found bunks with kids huddled together, scared. One of them was crying. Twelve of them total.

"It's okay," Sofia said. Her voice was softer now. "You're safe."

Ray helped get the kids out and they followed in silence. One girl grabbed his hand. She couldn't have been more than ten and he squeezed back.

Outside there were lights and ambulances. Social workers from his department had arrived. Ray handed the kids over and found Sofia talking to her captain. She saw him and walked over.

"You're an idiot."

"Yeah."

"Could have been killed."

"But I wasn't."

She looked at him. Something passed between them. Something neither of them could say. Not here. Not now.

"You should go home," she said.

"So should you."

"I've got paperwork. Reports."

"Coffee first?"

She hesitated. "Yeah. Coffee."

---

They went to a diner on Sunset that was open all night. They sat in a booth by the window and ordered coffee. It was better than station coffee but not by much.

"You think about it?" she asked. "Going back?"

"To being a cop?"

"Yeah."

"No. That part of my life is over."

"You're good at it. Saw you in there and the instincts are still there."

"The instincts got covered up for a while by the pills."

"But you're clean now."

"Doesn't change what I did."

She was quiet and drank her coffee.

"The boy you hit," she said. "I read the file. He survived."

"With brain damage. Can't walk right and can't talk right."

"But alive."

"Doesn't make it okay."

"No. It doesn't."

They sat in silence. Ray watched her hands around the coffee cup, strong hands with no ring. She had a small scar on her left wrist that disappeared under her sleeve.

"You grew up in LA?" he asked.

"East LA. Boyle Heights."

"Rough neighborhood."

"I survived."

"More than survived."

She smiled, small and tired.

"I worked hard and kept my head down. Made detective at thirty-two and lieutenant at thirty-eight."

"Impressive."

"I wanted it. Wanted out of that neighborhood and wanted to matter."

"You matter."

She looked at him and the tiredness in her eyes was deeper than just one long night.

"Sometimes I wonder. We take down one operation and another one pops up. The kids we saved tonight, where do they go and what kind of life do they get?"

"Better than the one they had."

"Maybe."

Ray's phone buzzed with a text from his supervisor asking questions about Tommy and the case. He put it away.

"I should let you go," Sofia said. "You've got work."

"So do you."

"Yeah."

Neither of them moved. Ray noticed the way the diner's light caught her eyes and made them less tired.

"Can I call you?" Ray asked. "When this isn't a case anymore?"

She looked at him for a long moment.

"I don't think that's a good idea."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm a lieutenant and you're a witness. It wouldn't be appropriate."

"After the trial then."

"Ray."

"What?"

"I don't mix work with anything else."

She was right and he knew it. But he also knew the way she looked at him when she thought he wasn't watching.

"One dinner," he said. "After the trial and after everything's settled."

"Ray."

"One dinner."

She stood and put money on the table.

"We'll see."

She left. Ray sat there and watched her walk to her car, watched the way she checked her surroundings even in a parking lot at dawn. Watched her drive away. He finished his coffee. It had gone cold.

---

The trial took three months. Ray testified and told the truth. He described what he saw at the house, the tattoos, the men with guns, the children locked upstairs. The defense attorney asked about his background.

"You were a police officer?"

"Yes."

"But you're not anymore."

"No."

"Why is that?"

"I was injured on the job and became addicted to pain medication. I left the force."

"So you're a former addict testifying about a drug operation."

"I'm a social worker who found three trafficked children."

The attorney tried other angles but Ray's testimony held. He'd seen what he'd seen and the physical evidence backed him up. The children, the equipment in the shed, the ballistics from the shots fired.

Sofia testified too, professional and precise. She described the raid and the warehouse and the twelve children they'd recovered. Her testimony was devastating.

The gang members got life, all of them. The kids went into the system. Ray followed up on Tommy and got him placed with a good family in Pasadena. He visited once a month.

He didn't call Sofia and she didn't call him.

Then one night his phone rang at three in the morning. Her number.

"Ray."

Just his name and he was awake.

"What's wrong?"

"We found him. Hector Vega, the head of Los Trece. He's at a compound in Pomona with hostages and kids inside."

"When are you going in?"

"Six a.m. I wanted to tell you."

"Be careful."

"I will." She paused. "Ray, after this is done, about that dinner."

"Yeah?"

"I think we should try it. See what happens."

"I'd like that."

"Okay. I'll call you after."

She hung up.

Ray couldn't sleep. He got up and made coffee and watched the news. Nothing yet. At seven his phone rang again. Not Sofia. Her captain.

"Mr. Tillman, Lieutenant Martinez asked me to call you if anything happened."

"What happened?"

"We got the location. She wanted you to know."

The captain gave him the address. An industrial park in Vernon. Ray wrote it down.

"Is the operation over?"

"Not yet. She thought you might want to be here when we bring the kids out."

Ray drove to Vernon. The industrial park was cordoned off with squad cars and tactical vehicles everywhere. He found a perimeter officer and explained who he was. They let him wait behind the line.

The operation took two hours. Ray stood by his car and watched officers move in and out of the building. Finally they started bringing children out. Eleven of them. Thin and scared and blinking in the daylight.

Sofia came out with the last group. She saw Ray and walked over. She'd taken off her helmet and her hair was pulled back and there was dust on her face. She looked tired but she smiled.

"You came."

"You asked me to."

"We got ten of them but Vega slipped out a back exit. We've got units searching the area."

"The kids are safe. That's what matters."

"Yeah." She looked at the children being loaded into ambulances. "That's what matters."

Her radio crackled. A voice said they'd spotted Vega heading east on foot toward the rail yards.

"I need to go," Sofia said.

"I'll wait."

She nodded and started to turn, then stopped. "Ray, I'm glad you're here."

She went back to her team.

Ray waited another twenty minutes. Then he heard sirens moving fast and more radio chatter. Something was wrong. He got in his car and drove toward the rail yards.

He found the scene by following the police cars. An old warehouse with broken windows and graffiti on the walls. Officers had the perimeter and Sofia was talking to her team by the entrance.

Ray parked and got out. One of the officers tried to stop him but Sofia saw him and waved him through.

"You shouldn't be here," she said.

"Seems like I never should be anywhere I end up."

"Vega's inside. He's cornered but he's armed."

"Let me help."

"How?"

"I can talk to him. I'm not a cop. Maybe he'll listen."

Sofia looked at him. "No. Too dangerous."

"Sofia."

"No."

A gunshot came from inside the warehouse and everyone ducked. Then a voice shouted from a broken window.

"I want a car. I want it now or I start shooting!"

Sofia grabbed her radio. "Negotiator's twenty minutes out."

"We don't have twenty minutes," her captain said.

Ray started walking toward the warehouse.

"Ray, stop!" Sofia called after him.

He didn't stop. He walked to the entrance with his hands up and called out.

"Hector Vega! My name is Ray Tillman. I'm not armed and I'm not a cop. I want to talk."

Silence. Then the voice again.

"Come in. Slow. Hands where I can see them."

Ray went inside. The warehouse was dim with light coming through the broken windows. He saw Vega at the far end behind some crates with a gun in his hand.

"That's far enough," Vega said.

Ray stopped. "You're done, Hector. The kids are safe and your operation is finished."

"I'm not going to prison."

"You don't have a choice."

"I've always got a choice."

"So make the right one. Put down the gun and walk out."

Vega laughed. "You think I'm stupid? You think I don't know what life in prison means for someone like me?"

"Better than dying here."

"Is it?"

Ray heard footsteps behind him. Soft but getting closer. He knew it was Sofia and her team moving in.

"Listen," Ray said louder. "Those kids you had, they're going to get a chance now. A real chance at life. You can't take that back."

"I don't care about those kids."

"I think you do. I think you're human enough to care."

Vega raised the gun and pointed it at Ray.

"You don't know anything about me."

The tactical team came through the side door fast and Vega turned. He fired and ran for the back exit. Officers returned fire but he was already moving.

Ray ran after him.

"Ray, no!" Sofia shouted.

He didn't stop. His leg screamed but he pushed through the pain. He went out the back door and saw Vega running between old rail cars. Ray followed.

Vega turned and fired twice. The shots went wide and he kept running. Ray was closing the distance. His old police instincts had taken over and he wasn't thinking, just moving.

Vega ducked between two containers and Ray followed. It was a dead end. Vega turned with the gun raised but it clicked empty. He dropped it and pulled a knife.

"Stay back," Vega said.

Ray held up his hands. "It's over, Hector."

"It's not over."

Vega came at him fast. Ray tried to dodge but his bad leg gave out and he fell. Vega was on him and the knife came down. Ray felt it go in below his ribs, cold and sharp. Then again in his side.

Gunshots cracked the air and Vega fell back. Sofia was there with her rifle up. Other officers came running. They secured Vega and called for an ambulance.

Sofia knelt beside Ray. "Stay with me."

"I'm okay."

"You're not okay." She pressed her hands against the wounds. Blood was spreading fast across his shirt.

"Sofia."

"Don't talk. Just stay with me."

The ambulance was ten minutes out. Sofia couldn't wait. She got officers to help carry Ray to her car and she drove. She ran every red light and drove on sidewalks and called ahead to the hospital.

Ray was in the passenger seat. The pain was bad and getting worse. He could feel the blood, warm and wet.

"Talk to me," Sofia said. "Stay awake."

"I'm here."

"Tell me about tomorrow. About dinner."

"Italian place. Silver Lake."

"What else?"

"Wine. Good wine."

"Keep talking."

But it was getting hard to talk and the edges of his vision were going dark. He looked at Sofia and her jaw was set and her knuckles were white on the steering wheel.

"I'm sorry," he said.

"Don't. Don't you dare."

"Tell Tommy. Tell him I tried."

"You're going to tell him yourself."

They got to the hospital and there were doctors waiting. They pulled Ray from the car and put him on a gurney. Sofia ran beside them holding his hand.

At the doors they stopped her. "You can't go further."

"I'm not leaving him."

"You have to."

They took Ray through the doors. Sofia stood there and watched them disappear. Her hands were covered in his blood.

A nurse came out an hour later. "Are you family?"

"No. I'm. We're." Sofia stopped. "How is he?"

"He's in surgery. The doctor will update you when there's news."

Sofia sat in the waiting room. Other officers arrived. Ray's supervisor from Child Services came. They all waited.

The surgeon came out at dawn. His scrubs were stained and his face was tired.

"We did everything we could. He lost too much blood and the damage to his organs was too severe. I'm sorry."

Sofia stood up. "Can I see him?"

"Of course."

She went to the room. Ray was on the bed, pale and still. They'd cleaned him up and removed the tubes. He looked like he was sleeping.

Sofia sat beside him and took his hand. It was already getting cold.

"I'm sorry," she said.

Her voice was level and her face was calm. She didn't cry. That would come later, alone in her apartment. For now she just sat and held his hand.

A nurse came in. "Take your time."

Sofia nodded. She stayed another twenty minutes and then she let go of his hand and stood up. She walked out of the hospital and got in her car. The passenger seat still had his blood on it.

She drove home and parked and sat in the driver's seat. The sun was coming up and the city was waking up around her.

Tomorrow she'd go back to work. Another operation. Another case. Another chance to make a difference.

Tonight she sat in her car.

---

The funeral was three days later. Ray didn't have much family. A sister in Oregon who came down. Some colleagues from Child Services. The church wasn't full but the people who came mattered.

Sofia sat in the front row and gave a eulogy. She kept it short and factual. Ray had been a good cop and then lost his way and then found it again. He'd helped a lot of kids and saved lives. He'd died doing what he thought was right.

She didn't mention their dinners or the plans they'd made. That was private.

After, Sofia went to the cemetery and stood by his grave. The headstone was temporary until the real one arrived. His name and his dates. Forty-six years.

"You were supposed to have more time," she said.

The wind picked up and she stood there for a while. Then she walked back to her car and drove to her apartment.

She had Ray's file on her desk. The one from when he was still a cop. She read through it again and saw the officer he'd been. Dedicated. Sharp. Good at the job.

Then she read the incident report from the accident. The toxicology report. The boy's medical records. She saw how everything had fallen apart.

And then she saw what he'd built back. Four years clean. A new career. Helping kids. Making amends.

She closed the file.

Tomorrow she'd get a call about another operation or another lead. The work never stopped and the city never ran out of broken things.

Sofia got up and poured herself a drink. She stood at the window and looked out at LA spreading below her in the night.

She thought about Ray showing up at the warehouse when she'd asked him to. About him running into that building unarmed. About him chasing Vega when he should have stayed back.

He'd been trying to save someone. Maybe Vega. Maybe himself.

She finished her drink.

The city went on below her, loud and indifferent. Full of people trying to do their best with what they had.

Ray had been one of them.

And for a little while, he'd been hers.
