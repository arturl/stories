# The Chicago River

Omar Al-Rashidi knows this city better than most people who were born in it. He came at four years old, in the back of his mother's life, after the invasion took everything else. Now he's twenty-seven, guiding tourists up and down the Chicago River on a boat, describing buildings he was going to build before the money ran out. He boxes twice a week in Albany Park. He draws at night at a table he tells people he doesn't use. He is, in every way, a man waiting for his real life to begin.

Then a red-haired woman misses his tour. She comes back the next day. She asks about the river — how deep it is, whether things stay where they fall.

What follows is a story about a city that keeps secrets, a love that was never entirely safe, and a man who helps cover up a murder without knowing that's what he's doing — until it's far too late to matter. Omar is smart, careful, and completely out of his depth. By the time he understands what Nora Callery has done, and what she's made him part of, he has already lost everything except the one thing he can still choose: who he protects on his way down.

*The Chicago River* runs through the heart of the city going the wrong direction. It has run that way for over a hundred years. If you didn't know the history, you'd never guess.
