# The Dogs Of Kolkata

## Act One — The Dogs' Hour

Sutanuti, Calcutta. June 1946.

In the early morning, the lanes of Chitpur Road belong to the desi dogs. They come out early, 
before chai wallahs, before the first electric trams start. 

They know the neighborhood and move without hurry, not wasting time or energy. They remember which doorstep, which 
courtyard yielded something before, and might again.

A dog sniffs the sidewalk where a street merchant sold biryani yesterday, then moves on. Another sits at the steps of
the police station, waiting for something that has a smell if not the shape. The constable 
will not arrive for another two hours.

The dogs are of different sizes and colors but look the same: slim build, pie-dish ears and a curved tail.
The street has produced a breed optimized for survival. 

On the wall near the police station, a Muslim League poster. The paper is still wet. The dogs pass
it without interest.

The streets come to life in a few hours: kathi rolls, jhal muri and chai. Until then, only the smell of the river Hooghly, a mile west, reaches here. The air is stifling. The monsoon will not arrive for another week.
