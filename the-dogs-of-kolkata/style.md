# The Dogs Of Kolkata — Style Guide

## Voice

Simple but not primitive.
Concise but not cryptic — it says what needs to be said to paint the picture.
Clear and visual. The reader should be in the place and time.
Meditative and explorative. This is not a thriller. The prose can breathe.

## Sentence Structure

Nouns first, then verbs.
Adjectives earn their place or they are cut.
Adverbs almost never.
Short sentences are allowed. Fragments are allowed when they serve the image.
No non-standard grammar.

## What the Prose Does Not Do

Does not explain what the image already shows.
Does not tell the reader how to feel.
Does not use two adjectives when one will do.
Does not use a long word when a short one is exact.
Does not announce its own intentions.

## What the Prose Does

Trusts the specific detail — a street name, a food, a sound, a smell.
Lets the reader do work. Not everything is explained.
Moves unevenly through time — a morning can take twenty pages,
a decade can pass in three sentences.
Places the reader in the scene before placing characters in it.

## On Dogs

Dogs are not symbols to be decoded. They are presences.
They are not sentimentalized. They are not explained.
Their behavior carries meaning the prose does not state.

## Tense and Person

Present tense for scenes. Past tense handled carefully to avoid inconsistency.
Third person. Farah is the primary consciousness the reader inhabits.

## Reference Points

Saramago — weight given to ordinary things, the dog as witness.
Coetzee (*Disgrace*) — stripped prose, moral weight carried by action not commentary.
Hardy — a woman who survives, who builds, who is not rescued.

## What to Check in Review

- Are there adjectives that explain rather than show?
- Is tense consistent within a scene?
- Does any sentence tell the reader what to feel?
- Is there a simpler word available?
- Does the specific detail do its job — place, time, texture?
- Does the prose trust the reader?
